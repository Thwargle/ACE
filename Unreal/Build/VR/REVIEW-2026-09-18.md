## Gameplay and replication

- Crossbows use their authored hand placement and a measured stock/grip frame.
  Regression models include Assassin's Whisper, Zefir's Breath and the classic
  crossbow. Bow handling is separate.
- Atlatls and individually thrown weapons fire on trigger press. Their shared
  missile recovery uses the existing countdown. Thrown items use their own stack,
  their dominant hand, and the same heel-of-palm aim convention as the atlatl.
  The server now permits their free-aim collisions without a preselected target.
- Newly equipped arrows, darts and bolts use the authored missile flight
  placement and measured nock/tip before the first shot. The local duplicate of
  authoritative held ammunition remains hidden.
- The bow anchor has a lateral draw offset (10 cm by default, adjustable 0–20 cm)
  so aiming forward does not require drawing directly into the face.
- VR movement direction includes a tracking-origin/stick-only option in addition
  to head and controller direction.
- VR pose version 2 carries the authoritative body root plus head, hands,
  equipment and ammunition transforms. Observers interpolate against that root
  and reset on teleport/equipment changes. Version 1 VR recipients receive their
  original layout; retail clients receive no extension poses. Root/pose updates
  run at 20 Hz, with a 25 Hz server input limit. Nearby remote bows have a small
  string mesh (8 vertices / 4 triangles, within 15 m).
- Close-range melee checks the current authoritative body first and retains a
  bounded observed-body fallback. Melee crowding uses a VR stand-off distance.
- Spell-wheel availability requires a caster. Selecting a spell does not change
  stance.
- Deliberate two-hand use checks normal use range rather than forcing both hands
  through collision. It still requires peace mode, both hands forward and
  separated, a still hold, and withdrawal to rearm.
- Underwater terrain no longer triggers the opaque comfort fade against the
  water surface. Actual bed/wall collision remains effective.

Server changes are required for thrown damage and the extended replication.
The updated server is published to `Unreal/Saved/VRServer`; the running shard
has not been restarted. Its current-start log showed zero active sessions and

## UI, world selection and lifetime

- Damage text is unsigned; critical hits use `Crit!` before the value. YOU/TARGET
  labels are larger. The enemy vial derives its silhouette from retail art with
  smooth alpha instead of the old rough black-background mask.
- Native bitmap fonts retain their original assets. Fractional-scale glyph
  rendering avoids pixel snapping rather than replacing the font.
- Spells in Effect uses the retail 32-pixel row template, selected/unselected
  states, alphabetic ordering, strongest enchantment per category, separate name
  and duration columns, and the actual spell description. Timers advance between
  packets, truncate fractions and use `m:ss` or `h:mm:ss`; permanent effects have
  no duration label. Long names cannot paint into the timer or scrollbar.
- Vendor quantity uses actual merchandise stock and maximum stack size. A
  non-stackable helmet is limited to one; sold-out stock cannot be bought.
  Quantity is revalidated when stock refreshes and when sending any purchase,
  including a previously filled cart.
- Desktop and VR selection share wall occlusion. Terrain and building collision
  use the camera channel rather than the object-selection channel they ignore.
  A visible room's drawn walls also occlude selection when its distant movement
  collision is inactive; this does not require additional collision cooking.
- Deletion, teleport epochs and authoritative world snapshots clear stale
  selection/poses. Creature health packets must refer to a nearby object; the
  server also rejects observers beyond 192 AC units or in portal transit.
  Portal entry expires old world notices. Existing keep-ring removal already
  destroys departed world actors and clears queued effects/sounds/attachments;
  inventory and vendor records intentionally survive independently.

2. Ordinary occlusion queries off: user still saw clipping.
3. HISM culling comparison: remaining affected objects were scenery (trees,

UE 5.8 `MobileShadingRenderer::SetupMobileBasePassAfterShadowInit` supplies a

native frame-time cost still needs an awake-headset comparison.

## Performance changes and evidence

- Reuse UI name-index allocation capacity while rebuilding current membership
  and visibility. Empty keys are pruned; stale window results are not retained.
  Comparison switch: `ace.UI.ReuseLookupStorage`.
- Prepare each creature's conservative projectile bounds once per multi-segment
  trajectory. Reject definite misses before the unchanged detailed body test.
  Straight bolts avoid the preparation overhead.
  Comparison switch: `ace.VR.ProjectileBodyBounds`.
- Do not propagate an unused portal-camera vector through every terrain material
  for outdoor HMD micro-movements. Enabled doorway masks still receive the exact
  current camera. Comparison switch: `ace.Render.SkipUnusedPortalCamera`.
- Share sound-volume configuration reads within a frame. Changing a volume
  slider invalidates the cache immediately; ambient/effects gain and focus mute
  remain separate.
- Reuse camera and parent attachment lookups within each particle simulation
  update. Caches do not survive the call or cross emitters. Moving hands and
  world-space particles retain their original behavior. Comparison switch:
  `ace.Particles.ReuseEmitterFrames`. A 256-particle CPU microbenchmark measured
  0.0229 versus 0.0218 ms/update; this small result is not a total-FPS claim.
- Added finer HUD timing scopes and an `-ACEPerfCameraMotion` option to the offline
  performance scene for reproducible small camera translations.

Desktop offline Caul scene, 1920×1080, 32 synthetic NPCs and 121 terrain blocks:
the earlier warmed comparison changed mean frame time from 3.008 to 2.886 ms
(332.41 to 346.55 FPS), p95 from 3.416 to 3.293 ms, and p99 from 3.776 to 3.691 ms.
HUD time per refresh changed from approximately 0.451 to 0.358 ms. These are
synthetic desktop results, not a claim of live-world or headset performance.

A second paired capture held that scene constant and added the same small camera

0 to 1, mean frame time changed from 2.669 to 2.499 ms (374.63 to 400.17 FPS),

6.4% less mean frame time in this fixture. Visibility CPU time changed from
0.30 to 0.19 ms/frame and asynchronous uniform-expression updates from 0.29 to

added together as if they were serial frame time. Captures and exported timers

The projectile microbenchmark (32 bodies × 48 arc segments) changed from
0.850 to 0.066 ms including prepared bounds, with identical hit/earliest-time

## Texture quality

Through v45, Quest capped runtime DAT world textures at 512 pixels via
`ace.Texture.MaxWorldSize`, including 1024-pixel terrain composites. The user
requested full-resolution landscape textures. The terrain upload now bypasses
the object cap on both platforms and preserves the full retail 1024-pixel blend
as uncompressed BGRA8 with all 11 mip levels. Other object textures retain their
existing budget. UI and sky also retain authored sizes. Trilinear filtering is
unchanged; the earlier native anisotropic experiment increased shimmer. The ASTC
packaging target does not compress these transient runtime terrain textures.

A full RGBA square with all mip levels costs approximately 1.33 MiB at 512 or
5.33 MiB at 1024. Raising the cap doubles each dimension and quadruples memory
for affected textures, not FPS cost. The actual bandwidth/frame-time effect
requires a like-for-like headset capture. The landscape exemption is a requested
quality fix, not an FPS improvement. The rendering audit now reports unique
landscape texture dimensions, formats, mip counts and estimated allocation so the
memory tradeoff can be checked on the device.

## Shadow correction confirmed in the headset

The mobile forward shader chooses a cascade using view-space depth. The former
two-cascade split therefore moved across the ground as the player pitched their
head. A single 2048-pixel cascade over 40 m removes that internal quality boundary.
The user tested this live and reported: "Boundary gone; nearby detail looks good."
This is now the Android default in both runtime setup and Quest configuration;
PC retains its existing four-cascade setup. This uses more shadow-map memory
than two 1024 maps and is a quality/correctness fix, not a claimed FPS win.
Desktop and ES3.1/mobile-LDR rendered tests retain defined full-body shadows,
including a locally hidden body and a moving arm.

## Offline performance work after the headset was removed

Three CPU changes are enabled in the shared client without changing scene detail:

- Doorway geometry is immutable for a given landblock and world scale. Keep a
  bounded 256-entry LRU of its compact polygons instead of rebuilding it for
  each visibility query. Camera/frustum admission is still recomputed live;
  incomplete streaming results are never cached. No actors or mesh resources
  are retained. Cache copies prevent clipping from modifying the stored data.
  The 23-door fixture measured roughly 1.98 us per lookup before and 0.415 us
  after. Regression covers Yaraq, Rithwic, Caul, two world scales, reloads,
  partial streaming and changing sides of a doorway.
- Setup movement/collision/script metadata previously reread and unpacked the
  same DAT record for repeated queries. A separate 512-entry LRU now stores only
  those fields (including negative lookups). It is cleared on DAT invalidation.
  The fixture measured roughly 8.88 us before and 0.010 us after. Tests compare
  real DAT fields/shapes, fallback values, eviction and reload behavior.
- VR aiming, hands and ammunition queries previously copied the entire equipped
  item list repeatedly. Read only the first owned item matching the required
  slot/type instead, copying a single item when the existing API needs a value.
  Queries read current session state; no persistent pointer survives updates.
  The 400-object/20-equipped fixture measured 1.95 us before and 0.052 us after.
  Tests cover parent-only ammunition, ownership, consumption, unequip, deletion
  and logout. Existing rig/menu and stair/ceiling regressions also passed.

These are isolated lookup measurements on the PC, not whole-game FPS multipliers.
The largest remaining native costs are wider than these lookups: animation,
per-part transform/scene updates, draw preparation and GPU pixel work.

Rigid actor draw-command caching was also implemented behind
`ace.Render.CachedActorDraws`. **PC enables it; Android remains opt-in until
native frame times are measured.** Moving-body,
moving-arm and shadow captures match the reference renderer on desktop and
mobile preview. In an initial trace, mesh-command preparation fell from about
0.446 to 0.236 ms per world tick and dynamic-primitive submissions from 73 to 20.
Those are aggregated CPU work, not critical-path frame times. The isolated
1920x1080 ES3.1 Caul repeats were slower overall with caching:

- Pair C, baseline then cached: mean 3.005 -> 3.145 ms; p95 3.577 -> 3.598 ms.
- Pair D, cached then baseline: mean 3.196 -> 3.068 ms; p95 3.763 -> 3.685 ms.
- Desktop SM5, 2560x1440: mean 3.318 -> 3.264 ms; p95 4.270 -> 4.230 ms.

These initial results did not justify a default change. After correcting the
fixture camera (below), packaged desktop runs with the full crowd visible showed:

- Baseline then cached: mean 3.111 -> 2.799 ms; p95 3.792 -> 3.533 ms.
- Cached then baseline: mean 2.805 -> 3.105 ms; p95 3.580 -> 3.819 ms.
